# Intent: Club registration
Author: product owner. Status: draft.

## Problem
A club cannot create an account, cannot create its own teams, cannot add players to a team's roster, and cannot register those teams for a tournament. Today a director types each team in (club, team name, and division) or imports a spreadsheet. Coaches have no account. A tournament cannot turn rosters on or leave them off, so a recreational adult tournament cannot stay simple.

A director cannot set when registration for a tournament opens or closes, and a division has no cap on teams. Nothing keeps a team from registering after the window has shut, or from taking a spot in a division that is already full. Registering a team is the same as the team being in the tournament. The director cannot approve it separately, and a full division has no waitlist. The tournament does not list a single price for a team, and it cannot enable an optional roster or list a roster deadline. A club has no way to register more than one team. The director cannot mark a team as paid.

## Proposed outcome
A person signs up to act for a club, the way a club director does on SportsWrench. They are not invited. They choose that role, activate the account from an email, and then create the club. The club has an administrative email different from that person's email. They create that club's teams, and add players to each team's roster. A player has a name and a jersey. The roster stays on the team. The club registers those teams for a tournament. When that tournament has rosters enabled, each team's roster has to be filled out.

Rosters are optional, and the director turns them on per tournament. With rosters off, the tournament asks for no roster, so a recreational adult tournament stays simple. With rosters on, each team entered in the tournament has to have its roster filled out. That roster is the players on the team, each with a name and a jersey. The director then sets a roster deadline, and the tournament lists it. When that deadline passes, the director gets an email. The email says the roster deadline has passed, lists the teams that have not submitted their rosters, and includes a link to manage teams. That link opens those teams in the software, highlighted to show the roster was not submitted. From there the director can drop a team, override the missing roster, or take another action. This slice does not apply an automatic consequence.

The director sets, on the tournament, when registration opens and when it closes, and one price for a team. The registration open, the registration close, and the roster deadline are each a date and time in the tournament's time zone. The tournament shows the registration open and the registration close, so a visitor can see both. The tournament lists that price. A club has no limit on how many of its teams can be in the tournament. The division cap still applies. This slice handles no payment. Collecting payment is a later slice. The director can mark a team as paid, for tracking. That later slice records the same paid mark when a team pays. Each division has a cap of teams. While registration is open, a club can register more than one team, each in a division. Every team is at that same price. A lower price for a club's later teams is a later slice. While the division is under its cap, that registration holds a spot and waits for the director. Once the division is at its cap, further registrations go on a waitlist, in the order they registered. A waitlisted team does not hold a spot, so the number of teams registered can be higher than the cap.

A team the director adds to a division, including a team from an import, counts toward that division's cap. When the teams being added would put the division over its cap, the director is asked whether to increase the cap. If the director raises the cap enough to include those teams, they are added. If the director does not raise the cap, those teams are not added.

After registration has closed, a club can no longer register a team. The director can still add, remove, and update teams in the tournament. An add after the close still counts toward the division cap.

Registered and accepted stay separate, as they do on SportsWrench. The director approves a team before it is accepted into the tournament. A waitlisted team stays registered until the director approves it into an open spot. A spot opens when the director declines a registration that holds one, removes an accepted team, or raises the division cap past the teams that hold a spot. The director can then approve a waitlisted team into that spot.

Registration follows the patterns of SportsWrench, the service now called OTTO Event. On that service a club director signs up, activates by email, creates the club with a separate administrative email, and then creates teams. The event has an open and a close, a roster deadline, and a listed registration fee, each division has a maximum number of teams, registered and accepted are separate counts, a full division waitlists later teams, and the club director enters the event and assigns the club's teams to divisions.

## Affected users and systems
Club contacts, who cannot do this today. Directors, whose tournaments currently receive teams only when the director adds or imports them, and who have no registration window, division cap, or roster switch to set. Recreational adult tournaments, which need to run without a roster. The accepted product intent, which says directors and organizers are the only writers and coaches, players, and spectators have no account. The existing club and team records, which a director creates by name and which persist across tournaments inside one organization. Sign-in, which is invite-only for directors and organizers. A club account, which a person creates for themselves. The tournament, which already has a name, dates, and a time zone, and has no registration open or close, roster switch, roster deadline, or team price. Divisions, which were left without a max-teams count for a later slice.

## Constraints
- A club is not an organization. An organization is the tenant that runs tournaments. A club is an affiliation that owns teams.
- A person creates a club account by signing up and choosing to act for a club. They activate it from an email. They are not invited. Director and organizer sign-in stays invite-only. The club has an administrative email different from that person's email. The person then creates the club's teams.
- A team is the long-lived record owned by a club. A club creates its teams. Entering that team in one division of one tournament is separate from the team itself.
- A club adds players to a team's roster. A player has a name and a jersey. The roster stays on the team.
- The registration open and the registration close are set on the tournament. Each is a date and time in the tournament's time zone. The tournament shows both, so a visitor can see them. The team cap is set on the division.
- The director sets one price for a team, and the tournament lists it.
- Rosters are off unless the director enables them on the tournament. With rosters off, no roster is collected. This keeps a recreational adult tournament simple.
- When rosters are on, each team entered in the tournament has to have its roster filled out: the players on that team, each with a name and a jersey. The director sets a roster deadline, and the tournament lists it. That deadline is a date and time in the tournament's time zone. When it passes, the director gets an email that lists the teams that have not submitted their rosters and links to manage teams. Those teams are highlighted in the software as roster not submitted. The director can drop a team, override the missing roster, or take another action. This slice does not apply an automatic consequence.
- This slice handles no payment. Collecting payment is a later slice. The director can mark a team as paid, and can clear that mark, for tracking. The mark does not accept the team, change its spot, or change its price. The later slice records that same mark when a team pays.
- A club can register more than one team for a tournament while registration is open. Every team is at that same price. A club has no limit on how many of its teams can be in the tournament. After registration has closed, a club cannot register a team.
- After registration has closed, the director can still add, remove, and update teams in the tournament. An add, including an import, still counts toward the division cap.
- A lower price for a club's later teams is a later slice.
- A registration holds a spot until the division reaches its cap. Registrations after that go on the waitlist, in the order they arrived, and do not hold a spot.
- A team the director adds or imports counts toward the division cap. When that would put the division over its cap, the director is asked whether to increase the cap. Raising it enough to include the teams adds them. Leaving the cap where it is does not add them.
- Registered and accepted are separate. The director approves a team before it is accepted into the tournament. Approval of a waitlisted team takes an open spot.
- Declining a registration that holds a spot, removing an accepted team, or raising the division cap past the teams that hold a spot opens a spot.

## Open questions
- One sign-in for the same person, who may run tournaments, act for a club, or both, versus two separate sign-ins.
- Which actions besides drop and override the director can take for a team whose roster was not submitted.
- How a club's teams relate to teams a director has already entered by typing a club name.
